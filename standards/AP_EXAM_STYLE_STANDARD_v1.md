# Med Daddy — A&P Exam-Style Question Standard (v1.0)

**Applies to:** All Anatomy & Physiology / A&P II Med Daddy units and future question-bank revisions (Urinary, Lymphatic, Endocrine, Male Reproductive, etc.).

**Purpose:** Match the short, direct, high-discrimination wording of authentic instructor review/mock questions while preserving scientifically sound application and Med Daddy's existing answer-quality rules.

## 1. Exam-style voice and stem design

- **Start with the concept being tested.** Most stems should be concise and exam-like; do not turn an anatomical, hormonal, or physiological distinction into a long fictional scene without a reason.
- Favor prompts such as "Which process ...?", "Where does ... occur?", "What changes when ... increases?", "Which structure/hormone ...?" and "Which statement best distinguishes ...?".
- Test **terminology, directional relationships, sequences, location, function, quantities, and cause/effect**, as well as brief applications of these facts.
- Preserve source-specific thresholds, numbers, diagrams, or qualifiers *when verified*; mark approximations as approximate and do not manufacture precision.
- A clinically framed stem is appropriate when it genuinely tests A&P physiology; it is **not** mandatory for every A&P question. PCT units retain a stronger paramedic-assessment/treatment emphasis.
- Closely mirror the **style and objective** of source review/mock material but **do not reproduce anyone's private question wording** or key in public sets without their approval.

## 2. Defensible distractors — mandatory

Every item has exactly four choices and **one defensible best answer**. Each alternative must:

1. Answer the question actually being asked (e.g., if the stem asks for an *organ*, all choices should be organs; if it asks for a *mechanism*, all choices should be mechanisms).
2. Be the **same grammatical form and semantic category** as the keyed answer.
3. Be **plausible to a student who almost knows the concept**, not obviously silly, off-topic, or trivially impossible.
4. Have comparable specificity, length and plausibility to the correct answer; the keyed choice should not be conspicuously longer or more qualified.
5. Reflect a real confusion the instructor might test: secretion vs reabsorption; ADH vs aldosterone; filtration vs GFR; pH vs bicarbonate; interstitial vs intracellular fluid; artery vs vein, etc.
6. Avoid easy giveaways through ungrounded absolutes (*always*, *never*, *only*, *every*) or an obviously unrelated subsystem.
7. Include an **individually authored explanation of why each wrong option fails** on this particular stem.

Avoid answer leakage: do not put the key in a title/heading, colon/dash prefix, stem, metadata exposed during practice, or conspicuous option design. Stem, key, source, explanation and wrong-answer rationales must all agree.

## 3. Difficulty calibration

Use a mix of:
- **Direct knowledge / recognition**: anatomically precise facts, terms and locations; genuinely plausible alternatives.
- **Differentiation / close choices**: compare neighboring structures, processes, hormones, ions, steps and forces.
- **Short application**: one clinical or physiological change; predict the direction or mechanism.
- **Numerical / sequence**: source-verified ratios, flow paths and relevant calculations.

**Do not set a rigid percentage from one exam result.** Adapt the balance to each instructor review, marked topic and actual unit. Do not confuse "longer stem" with "harder question." A short question can be instructor-level difficult if its choices are well written.

## 4. Med Daddy coverage standards remain unchanged

- Read every uploaded lecture, handout, diagram, highlighted point, textbook chapter and review sheet **before** writing source claims.
- Map each numbered instructor review-sheet point and all subparts.
- **Five independently meaningful questions per review point** for a fully complete unit (the focused Urinary Review 50 currently contains two per point and is *not* completion evidence).
- **Three separately authored questions per explicit question embedded in a slide** where applicable.
- Maintain an **independent High-Yield 50** with ten critical concepts × five meaningfully different items when required for the unit.
- Separate original source mocks, their verbatim wording, supplied keys, and any uncertain source answers from newly authored practice.
- Provide standard 10/20/50/full sessions, explanations after questions, and missed-plus-new adaptive practice without compromising existing progress.
- Do not silently import another person's or the user's private mock content into public Med Daddy. Use its *stylistic lessons*, not the private word-for-word test.
- Source authority: the user's uploaded A&P courses/textbooks. Use Ontario PCP BLS/ALS only where A&P questions touch paramedic action; do not mistake older A&P lecture treatment examples for current PCP authorization.

## 5. QA acceptance gate before shipping new A&P banks

- [ ] Every objective and required review subpart mapped to specific item IDs
- [ ] Four sensible same-category choices; exactly one keyed best answer
- [ ] Source, key and all four explanations independently checked
- [ ] No answer in the stem, heading, or preceding labels
- [ ] No giveaway option length, absolutist distractors, or absurd alternatives
- [ ] No duplicate or near-duplicate stems / concepts masquerading as separate questions
- [ ] Source numbers and mechanisms verified; no unsourced claims
- [ ] Realistic study mode quiz launch and answer take-up tested at 10, 20, 50 and full bank
- [ ] Independent old-bank rollback; no surprise UI redesign or storage/progress reset
- [ ] State precisely which coverage gates are still incomplete

## 6. Implementation note

This document is a **quality standard for A&P authoring and future audits**, not a claim that already published A&P question banks have been fully revised or tested. Apply it during subsequent A&P unit revisions and use the instructor's real mock/review patterns as the calibration benchmark.

**PCT remains separately weighted toward Ontario PCP assessment, treatment priorities, directive use and field decision making.**
